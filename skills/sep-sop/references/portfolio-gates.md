# Five-Month Portfolio Plan and Stage Gates

This plan is designed to test whether the 200+ target is economically and operationally attainable. It does not promise ranking, indexing, ad approval, or revenue. Define whether the target counts independent domains, branded sites, games, or indexable URLs before using the number.

## Before the Clock Starts

Record:

- Target unit and whether 200 is a hard cap, a stretch target, or a count of published assets.
- Target countries/languages, audience, game category, acquisition channel, and intended ad provider.
- Available build/review hours per week, domain/hosting/research budget, and maximum acceptable loss.
- Baseline success/stop criteria for each cohort. Choose thresholds before seeing results; do not retrofit them to make weak results look successful.
- Who owns license review, QA, deployment, analytics, ad approval, and incident response. One person may own several roles, but each responsibility must be explicit.

Translate the five-month target into a capacity model. Two hundred units over five months is about 40 per month or 9-10 per week. Measure hands-on hours per asset across research, rights checks, implementation, QA, release, and ongoing support. Sustainable weekly throughput is available hours divided by measured hours per asset, with explicit capacity reserved for defects, analytics, policy work, and maintenance. Estimate recurring cost separately for domains, hosting, data tools, and ad/analytics operations. If the measured capacity or budget cannot support the target, change the target unit or scale plan before launch.

## Stage 0: Foundation

Set up one portfolio ledger and one repeatable site template. Check that the analytics, Search Console, deployment, domain, and ad-account access needed for measurement can actually be obtained. Run the build, QA, and deployment workflow once end-to-end on a non-sensitive test asset. Record elapsed hands-on time and the real recurring costs.

Do not purchase domains, create public repositories, or deploy production sites as an implicit part of research. Obtain explicit authorization for each consequential external action.

## Stage 1: Small Pilot

Test a small number of genuinely distinct game/query hypotheses, preferably on a small number of coherent sites rather than buying a separate domain for every keyword. A practical starting cohort is 3-5 game concepts; adjust it to the user's time and budget. Each concept must pass rights review and the release checklist.

For every pilot, record:

- The query cluster and live SERP evidence that justify the page.
- The specific user need and the meaningful product difference from existing results.
- Source code/assets, licenses, required attribution, and monetization permissions.
- Build/QA/deployment time, one-time and recurring cost, and technical failures.
- Search Console/analytics setup and the expected date to review early signals.

Keep ads disabled until the provider's eligibility and placement requirements are met. Do not estimate revenue from search-volume figures alone.

## Stage 2: Cohort Review

Review each cohort on a fixed cadence (weekly operational health; monthly portfolio decision is a reasonable default). Keep these outcomes separate:

- **Technical:** deployed, available, playable, mobile/keyboard usable, error-free.
- **Discovery:** submitted, crawled, indexed, impressions, clicks. Submission is not indexing; indexing is not ranking.
- **Engagement:** starts, meaningful plays, repeat play, or another validated interaction event.
- **Monetization:** provider approval, policy status, eligible sessions/impressions, revenue, and revenue per session/page when available.
- **Economics:** build cost, recurring domain/hosting/tool cost, maintenance time, and contribution after direct costs.

For each metric, show the reporting window, denominator, data source, and coverage. Mark unavailable data as unavailable, not zero. Do not compare different countries, devices, page types, or incomplete windows as if they were equivalent.

## Stage 3: Expand, Hold, or Stop

Expand a pattern only when it has repeatable evidence across more than one asset or cohort, passes all rights and quality checks, has usable measurement, and its incremental cost is acceptable. Expansion should copy the validated process, not duplicate near-identical content.

Hold and diagnose when the sample is too small, indexing is still immature, tracking is missing, or a technical/policy issue obscures the result. Improve the existing asset before adding more clones.

Stop an asset or pattern when rights are unclear, it fails user-value or ad-policy checks, repeated technical defects remain unresolved, or predeclared economic/quality stop criteria are breached. Keep the outcome in the ledger so the same failure is not repeated.

## Five-Month Cadence

Use months as decision windows, not quotas:

1. **Month 1:** establish the measurement/deployment baseline and run the pilot.
2. **Month 2:** repair weak points; continue only the concepts with credible early discovery or engagement evidence.
3. **Month 3:** replicate validated patterns in a controlled cohort; compare build time and per-asset outcomes.
4. **Month 4:** concentrate effort on the strongest market/game/query clusters; retire failing patterns.
5. **Month 5:** report cumulative assets, indexation and traffic evidence, monetization status, costs, operational load, and whether the 200-unit target remains justified.

If evidence does not support scaling, the reliable result is to stop short of 200 and explain why. The goal is to avoid multiplying an unvalidated assumption.
